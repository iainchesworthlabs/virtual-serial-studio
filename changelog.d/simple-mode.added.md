**Simple mode is a guided list of jobs.** Studio opens on "What do you want to do?" with the jobs in plain words: give a program a serial port, connect two programs, use or share a real device, try a device from this window, let other computers use a port, put several programs on one line, send several into one, and let programs take turns. Each job is one form with a picture of what it builds and a sentence saying what will happen. Each setup you make has its own page that says which port to open in which program, shows whether a program has opened it, warns when its baud rate differs from the device's, and lets you type to the device and watch the data go through. You can change, add or close a program from that page, and **Remove setup** closes a pair, a share or an attached device with all of its ports in one step. Making several virtual ports at once (up to 32) numbers the names for you, four bundled setups and your recent ones are one click away under **Setups**, and the window warns before it takes a port away from a program that has it open. On Windows the COM numbers a form offers are free ones: it skips the numbers of ports that are plugged in, in your setups and in other rows, and numbers Windows keeps for ports that were unplugged or removed. If Windows still refuses one, the form says which COM number clashed and moves on to a free one.
