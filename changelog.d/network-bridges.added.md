**Bridge a serial port to the network, as a client or a server.** `tcp://`, `udp://` and `rfc2217://` endpoints are first-class ports, and any endpoint can be split across several listeners or merged from several publishers. `tcp-listen://host:port` accepts any number of TCP clients and sends them everything written to it, `udp-listen://host:port` reads datagrams from anyone and replies to the senders it has heard from lately, and `rfc2217-listen://host:port` makes Studio an RFC 2217 server: a serial-over-network client such as pySerial's `rfc2217://` can open it as a serial port, the client's DTR and RTS reach the linked port as control lines, and the modem lines come back to the client. (The baud rate and framing a client asks for are accepted but are not applied to a real device linked to the server.) The **Put a device on the network** dialog builds and checks these addresses, and `vss_cli bridge` takes the same forms. Every accepted TCP connection has Nagle's algorithm off, so a short reply on a polled serial bus is not held back. A dropped `tcp://` or `rfc2217://` client can be opened again automatically with `vss_cli run --reconnect`, after a delay that doubles from one second to thirty.
