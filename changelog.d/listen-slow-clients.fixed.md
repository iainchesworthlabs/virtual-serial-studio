**One client that stops reading no longer stops a listening port for the others.** A `tcp-listen://` or `rfc2217-listen://` port now keeps a queue for each client. A client that falls more than 1 MiB behind, or takes no data for 30 seconds, is disconnected and the log names its address; the other clients and the device carry on. Before, one client that stopped reading held back every client and filled the port's write queue until data from the device was dropped.

**A listening port has limits on what a client can make it do.** It accepts up to 32 clients at once and closes any more as soon as they connect, with a note in the log (at most one every 10 seconds). When the system cannot accept a connection, for example because it is out of file descriptors, the port waits 100 ms and then longer, up to 5 seconds, before it tries again, and says so once, instead of using a whole processor. An RFC 2217 client that sends requests and does not read the replies is disconnected when its replies reach the 1 MiB limit instead of growing memory without end.
