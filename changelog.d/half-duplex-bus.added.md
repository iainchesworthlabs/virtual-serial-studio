**A shared bus can be a half-duplex line, like a two-wire RS-485 pair.** Tick **Half-duplex line** on the Shared bus page of **Connect ports** (Advanced) or in the bus's settings, or add `"half_duplex": { "bit_rate": 9600 }` to a bus link in a topology file. The bus then has a line rate: a message is on the line for its bytes at that rate, and the other ports hear it when its last byte has left the line. A message that starts while another port's is still on the line, or inside the **turn-around** time after it, is counted and shown in the Monitor and the Log ("device-2 sent 6 bytes while device-1 was sending"). The messages that collided arrive as they were sent, or, if you choose **Report it and garble the bytes**, with every byte inverted and counted, so a checksum fails as it would on a real line. **Send only while RTS is high** gates a port by its RTS; in this build that works for loopback ports, and every other port always sends, because Studio does not learn of a program's RTS in step with its bytes. A bus without the option, and every other link, behaves and is saved exactly as before. A station's own echo is still not modelled.
