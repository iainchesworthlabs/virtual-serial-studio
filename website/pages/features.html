Features

Everything on this page is in the Community edition. The window has two levels. Simple is a list of your setups and a page of jobs. Advanced is a diagram with the panes an integrator or a diagnostician needs. Both show the same studio, and switching does not change what you have built.

About the pictures. The pictures on this page are made by the application drawing its own window off screen with sample ports, and they follow your light or dark theme. A dashed frame marks a picture that is not here yet.

Simple mode and virtual ports

Simple mode opens with your setups and a page of jobs. A job is a short form with defaults. The result is a port with a name to use in your program.

  • A virtual port, or a linked pair so that two programs talk to each other.
  • On Windows, a COM port from Studio's own driver, with the COM number you choose. Old programs that only offer COM1 to COM4 can have one of those.
  • On Linux and macOS, a pseudo terminal with a stable name.
  • “Type to” and “Data going through” on each setup's page, so you can test a port without another program.

Sharing a device

The operating system lets one program open a serial port. Studio opens the device and gives each program its own port, so several programs can use the device at once.

  • Split one device to several listeners, or join several publishers into one device.
  • Choose the owner: the one program allowed to write. Bytes from the others are counted and dropped, and the Monitor shows it.
  • Put several programs on a shared bus with a silent tap, and model RS-422 and half-duplex RS-485 lines.
  • See which program is on the other side of a port, where the system can tell.

Monitor and capture

A watcher is a read-only copy of a port. It never transmits and never drives a control line.

  • A row for each transfer: time, port, direction, byte count and a preview as text or hex.
  • Pause, follow, filter by port and direction, and search the payload.
  • Export a capture as .cap, .csv or .jsonl. A capture holds every byte with the time the engine moved it, on one clock for every port.
  • Send exact bytes as text or hex, once or on a repeat, and keep the sends you use often.

Advanced mode

Advanced mode is a canvas with a toolbox. Drag ports onto it and draw the links. The diagram shows the state of every port and link.

  • Ports, Inspector, Send and Monitor panes that you can arrange, and a command palette that finds any action.
  • Undo and redo for the edits you make on the diagram.
  • Counters for bytes and drops, the state of every control line, and the raw address of each port.
  • A throughput benchmark that reports what was sent, received and lost.

Network endpoints

A network endpoint is a port like any other, so it can be shared, watched and linked in the same way.

  • TCP, RFC 2217 and UDP, as a server that listens or a client that connects.
  • Bring a device that is plugged into another computer onto your diagram.
  • Control lines and break from an RFC 2217 client are passed on.
  • You choose the address to listen on and who may connect. Nothing listens until you make an endpoint.

These protocols carry no encryption and no login of their own. The user guide explains how to keep an endpoint safe.

Testing without hardware

Studio can stand in for a device, slow a link down and inject line faults, set by hand in the window.

  • A mock fixture stands in for a device: it echoes, or generates text, a fixed pattern, a counter or random bytes at the rate you set.
  • Delay and jitter on a link.
  • DTR, RTS, CTS, DSR, DCD and RI: see them, drive them, and choose how a link wires them.
  • Inject a parity error, a framing error, an overrun, a break, a hang-up or a disconnect on a fixture.

The Pro plugins will let a test harness set these during a run.

Setups and the command line

A setup is a JSON file that you can save, open and start from a template. Export as command gives the equivalent vss_cli command, which runs on a machine with no display.

  • capture records every byte to a file until you stop it.
  • play writes a capture back into a port at the recorded pace, faster, slower or in a loop.
  • respond makes a port answer like a device from a script of rules, with a delay, jitter and a seed.
  • Close the window and the ports stay up. Studio waits in the notification area until you quit it.
vss_cli share serial://COM3 --listeners=3
vss_cli capture --uri=serial://COM5 --out run.jsonl
vss_cli play bus.cap --uri=serial://COM6 --speed=2
vss_cli respond device.json --uri=serial://COM7
vss_cli bridge serial://COM3 tcp-listen://0.0.0.0:4001

Diagnostics

Three tools report what is wrong when nothing arrives.

  • Setup check lists what this machine needs and what is missing, with a fix for each.
  • “Why is nothing arriving?” checks each step between the device and your program and suggests what to try.
  • A diagnostic bundle is a ZIP with the setup, the event log, the environment and the port counters. Traffic contents are left out unless you tick them. Nothing is sent anywhere; you choose where to save the file.

In every edition

Three systems
Windows 10 and 11, Linux (packages for Debian, Ubuntu, Fedora and RHEL, and an archive) and macOS on Apple silicon. Built and tested on all three every night.
Light and dark
Both themes meet WCAG 2.2 AA contrast. Follow the system or choose one. Text size, high contrast and reduced motion follow your system.
Keyboard and screen readers
Every control is reachable by keyboard and has an accessible name and role. State is never shown by colour alone.
Nine languages
The window follows the language of the system, including right-to-left languages.
